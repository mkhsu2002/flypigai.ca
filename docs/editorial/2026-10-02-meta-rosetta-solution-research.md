## Editorial assessment

Three distinct articles are supportable in principle, but the public material supports one well-sourced overview now. The three routes are not interchangeable: visible-light pixel-level colour splitting, near-infrared receiving optics, and long-wave infrared thermal optics each require a different sensor and measurement context. The first article explains that distinction and gives Canadian product teams a qualification checklist.

Potential follow-ups, contingent on more evidence:

1. **NIR receiving optics and eye-tracking modules:** publish after obtaining a specific design's measured efficiency, field of view, distortion, module stack and integration data.
2. **LWIR thermal optics for compact systems:** publish after obtaining test images, detector compatibility, temperature/environment data and production evidence for a defined application.

Do not imply that the visible-light colour splitter is a complete camera lens, or that any of the three routes are qualified commercial modules based only on public descriptions. Do not repeat unverified savings, range or flight-endurance claims as measured outcomes.
